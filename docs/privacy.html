CipherSheet Privacy Policy

Last updated: June 17, 2026

This Privacy Policy applies to the CipherSheet Google Sheets™™ add-on and the CipherSheet website. It explains how CipherSheet accesses, uses, stores, and shares Google user data.

CipherSheet's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Who We Are

CipherSheet is an independent open-source project. Project homepage: https://www.yuvalsadan.com/CipherSheet/.

Plain-Language Terms

  • Google user data: data obtained through Google APIs used by CipherSheet.
  • Process: access, read, write, or otherwise handle data to provide app features.
  • Share or disclose: make data available to another party outside normal operation of Google services used by the app.
  • Retain: keep data until it is changed, deleted, or automatically expires.

Google User Data We Access

CipherSheet requests the minimum Google OAuth scopes needed for user-facing features.

  • auth/spreadsheets.currentonly: Read and write selected cell values in the current spreadsheet, and manage CipherSheet notes and cell editing protection.
  • auth/script.container.ui: Show CipherSheet sidebar and dialogs in Google Sheets™.
  • auth/userinfo.email: Identify your email address so CipherSheet can label your registered public key and let collaborators recognize you as a recipient.

How We Use Google User Data

CipherSheet only uses Google user data to perform the functionality provided by the add-on.

  • Encrypt plaintext entered by the user and write ciphertext to selected cells.
  • Decrypt ciphertext locally in the browser when a valid key is loaded.
  • Store add-on settings and your ECDH public key (used for asymmetric encryption by collaborators) in Apps Script document properties.

What We Do Not Do With Google User Data

  • We do not transfer Google user data outside Google Sheets™.
  • We do not sell Google user data.
  • We do not use Google user data for advertising (including targeted or interest-based advertising).
  • We do not transfer Google user data to data brokers, information resellers, or for credit or lending purposes.
  • We do not use Google user data to train generalized AI/ML models.
  • We do not allow humans to read your Google user data, except: (a) with your affirmative agreement for specific data; (b) where necessary for security purposes (such as investigating abuse); (c) to comply with applicable law; or (d) where the data has been aggregated and anonymized so it can no longer identify an individual user. In practice, because CipherSheet runs entirely in your browser and operates no backend service, no CipherSheet developer ever has access to your spreadsheet content, keys, or other Google user data.

Sharing and Disclosure

CipherSheet does not run its own backend service. CipherSheet does not store your spreadsheet content or keys.

  • We do not disclose Google user data to third parties for unrelated purposes.
  • Data needed for add-on functionality is processed through Google Sheets™ and Google Apps Script.
  • Spreadsheet collaborators can view spreadsheet content according to your Google Sheets™ sharing settings.
  • Unless explicitly approved by the user, plaintext data is kept on the client (user browser) and not communicated to Google Sheets™.

Storage, Retention, and Deletion

  • CipherSheet does not store spreadsheet content or Google user data on CipherSheet-owned servers.
  • Your private encryption key is generated and stored locally by your browser. It is never transmitted to any server. Your ECDH public key is stored in spreadsheet document properties so collaborators can encrypt data for you.
  • Temporary cache entries used for confirmation flow are short-lived and expire automatically.
  • Your private key is stored in your browser's IndexedDB (in the sidebar's googleusercontent.com origin) in encrypted form, protected by your unlock password via PBKDF2-SHA256 (600 000 iterations). If you enroll a passkey for unlock, a passkey credential ID and an encrypted copy of your unlock password (protected by the WebAuthn PRF output via HKDF-SHA256) are also stored in IndexedDB. Clearing your browser's site data for that origin will delete these stored key entries.
  • Passkey (WebAuthn PRF) unlock runs in a popup served from CipherSheet's own GitHub Pages site (www.yuvalsadan.com/CipherSheet). This popup exchanges only the data needed to unlock your local key and never receives your spreadsheet content or any Google user data.

You can delete data by clearing relevant cells, removing document properties, or deleting the spreadsheet file. Uninstalling the add-on does not automatically delete existing spreadsheet data.

Security Protections

  • Client-side encryption/decryption uses the browser Web Crypto API and AES-256-GCM.
  • Your private key is generated in your browser and never transmitted to any server. Your public key is registered in document properties to enable asymmetric encryption by collaborators. Your private key is not recoverable by CipherSheet if your unlock password is lost.
  • OAuth scopes are limited to what is required for user-facing functionality.

Cell Payload and Recipient Privacy

Each encrypted cell formula embeds a SHA-256 hash of each recipient's email address (lowercase) rather than the plaintext email. This keeps addresses out of the raw formula, but SHA-256 hashes of email addresses may be reversible by enumeration for a known address space (e.g. a corporate domain or small team). Anyone with read access to a cell's raw formula — including users with view-only sheet access — can see the recipient hash list for that cell, even if they cannot decrypt its content.
